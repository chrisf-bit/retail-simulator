# Handover crib: Ray Dolan, the unreliable narrator

Facilitator-only answer key for the store handover document in **The Store Is
Yours**. Do not show this to teams. The handover (the "Handover" button in the
team header, prepared by outgoing manager **Ray Dolan** of Plumfield Northgate)
is a deliberate unreliable narrator. Every opinion in it is confident, plausible
and self-serving, and it collides on purpose with what teams find on the floor.

The design intent: Ray's advice tempts teams to **act early on his word**.
Teams that take it at face value make confident wrong moves. Teams that **read
it against the floor and wait to observe** do better. Use this crib in the
debrief to name which opinions were traps, and pair it with the room-facing
questions in [debrief-questions.md](debrief-questions.md) (the handover section).

Golden rule to land: the handover is one person's view, not ground truth. A good
new manager treats it as a hypothesis to test, not an instruction to follow.

## How to use it

- Do not pre-empt it. Let teams discover the mismatches themselves across the shifts.
- In the debrief, ask the open handover questions first (what did you trust, where did it differ).
- Then, where useful, name one or two specific traps below to sharpen the point.
- Frame it as a leadership lesson, not a "gotcha". The skill is reading people and data for yourself.

## The store and priority traps

| What Ray says | Why it is a trap |
|---|---|
| "The dip on the dashboards is a seasonal blip and a data quirk. Do not chase it." | Primes teams to dismiss real KPI and hidden-driver movement as noise. The numbers are usually telling them something. |
| "Availability and service have always been our strength. Customers love this store." | A confident claim about the past that may be masking a present decline. Teams should verify, not inherit it. |
| First-week priorities: planogram compliance, push availability numbers, silence the WhatsApp grumbling. | Vanity and commercial optics over the safety, people and integrity signals that actually move the drivers. Note what Ray does not put on the list. |

## The master trap (the sign-off)

Ray signs off with: *"Hit the ground running, make a couple of visible calls in
your first week so they know who is in charge, and do not overthink it. Trust
your gut over the dashboards."*

This is the whole mechanic in one line. It rewards **premature, visible action**
over reading the store first. The teams who feel most flattered by it are the
ones most likely to act early and wrongly. Worth quoting back to the room in the
debrief: whose gut did they trust, Ray's or their own, and what did it cost or
save them?
