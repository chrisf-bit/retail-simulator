# Post-shift debrief question bank

Generic, reusable facilitator questions for the post-shift (round-results)
debrief in **The Store Is Yours**. All open, all phrased so they work after any
shift and do not reveal the scoring logic. Grouped by thread.

Usage: pick two or three per shift, not ten. Ask, then sit in the silence (the
second answer is usually the honest one). Follow up with "say more about that"
or "what makes you say that?" rather than moving straight to the next question.

## Opening / general reflection
- What just happened in that shift, in your own words?
- What are you most pleased with from that shift?
- What would you do differently if you ran that shift again?
- What surprised you?
- Where did the pressure bite hardest, and what did you do with it?
- What was the hardest single decision, and why?
- If you had to sum that shift up in one word, what would it be?
- What did that shift ask of you that the last one did not?

## Priorities and focus
- What did you decide to lead with this shift, and why that?
- What did you consciously choose *not* to focus on?
- How did you decide what could wait?
- Looking back, was your priority the right one for what the store actually needed?
- What pulled your attention first when the shift opened?
- Did your priority hold all shift, or did it move? What moved it?
- What would have had to be true for you to choose a different focus?

## Action and approach (act vs observe)
- Did you act quickly, or take time to read the situation first? How did that serve you?
- When did you decide you had enough information to act?
- Where did you jump in when you could have watched a little longer, or the reverse?
- What is the cost of waiting in a shift like that? What is the cost of acting too soon?
- Was there a moment you wish you had paused before acting?

## Leadership style
- How did you choose to lead your team this shift?
- What did you want your team to feel by the end of that shift?
- How do you think your team experienced the way you led?
- Did your style fit the shift, or did you default to how you usually lead?
- When did you feel the urge to take more control, or to let go more?
- What would leading that shift the opposite way have cost you?
- Which of your people needed a different approach from the rest?

## Resource allocation and trade-offs
- How did you decide where to put your people?
- What did you starve in order to feed something else?
- What was the trade-off you found hardest to make?
- If you had one more pair of hands, where would they have gone, and what does that tell you?
- Did your split of effort match the priority you set, or did they pull apart?
- What did you protect, even under pressure?

## The people moment
- What do you think that person actually needed from you?
- What were they not saying?
- How confident are you they left that conversation with what they needed?
- What did your response teach them about how to bring you a problem?
- Would you handle that person the same way if the store were calmer?
- What did attending to that person cost you elsewhere, and was it worth it?
- When one of your team comes to you mid-crisis, what is your instinct, and did it serve you here?

## Confidence and risk appetite
- How sure were you of your plan when you committed to it?
- What told you it was a shift to press forward, or one to hold back?
- When has backing yourself hard paid off, and when has it caught you out?
- What would you have needed to see to be bolder, or more cautious?
- Did your confidence match the situation, or your mood?
- How did the rest of the team feel about how much you were willing to stake?

## Disruptions and the unexpected
- When it broke, what was the first thing you did?
- How did the disruption change your plan, if at all?
- What did you drop to deal with it, and did you pick it back up?
- How did you keep the team steady when things went sideways?
- Did the disruption reveal anything about how you lead under real pressure?
- What did a shift with no disruption tempt you to do differently? (for clean shifts)
- How ready were you for something to go wrong before it did?

## Reading the store / instinct vs data
- What were the numbers telling you, and did you believe them?
- Where did your gut and the data disagree, and who won?
- What did you notice that the dashboard could not show you?
- What did you miss that was there to be seen?
- How much of your decision was evidence, and how much was instinct?

## The handover (the unreliable narrator)
- How much did you trust the handover you were given?
- Where did what you found differ from what you were told?
- What did the previous manager get right, and what did they not?
- At what point did you stop leaning on the handover and start trusting the floor?
- If you were writing the handover for the next manager, what would you be honest about that this one was not?

## Team dynamics (you shared the decisions)
- How did you make those calls as a group?
- Who ended up driving the decisions, and how did that happen?
- Whose view did not get heard, and what might you have missed because of it?
- How did you handle it when you disagreed?
- Did sharing the decision make it better, slower, or both?
- Was there a call you made together that no one individually would have made?
- How did the way you worked as a team change from the start of the shift to the end?

## Emotion and pressure
- Where did you feel the pressure most, physically or mentally?
- What was going through your head at the busiest moment?
- Did you stay the leader you wanted to be when it got hard?
- What tends to happen to your judgement when the pressure rises?
- How did you look after yourself, and each other, during that shift?

## Self-awareness and patterns (strong after later shifts)
- What is becoming a pattern in how you lead these shifts?
- What do you keep coming back to, shift after shift?
- What have you changed since the first shift, and why?
- What does this shift confirm about how you lead, and what does it challenge?
- Where are you growing more comfortable, and where are you still avoiding?
- If someone watched all your shifts back to back, what would they say your leadership signature is?

## Transfer to the real workplace
- Where have you seen a shift like that in your real store?
- What did that shift remind you of from your own team?
- What is one thing from that shift you will try differently on Monday?
- Who on your real team does that person you just handled remind you of?
- What would your own manager have made of how you led that?
- What is the smallest change from today that would make the biggest difference back at work?
